### Singleton rate → decision layer
- Only **5.58%** of S1s are singletons (identical in both train countries: 5.59 / 5.58). **94.4% of S1s have ≥1 match**, so an empty prediction costs 0.0 on almost every row: the "has any match" gate should be permissive. Precision matters *within* the list (mean 3.67 matches, 48% have 4+).
- Singleton near-misses are strong decoys: same or shuffled name, different address (e.g. "Continental Center LLC" vs "CONTINENTAL LLC CENTER", tsr=100, other city). **Name similarity alone is not enough; address agreement must gate matches.**

### One-owner rule: **SAFE**
- 0 of 7,638,365 matched S2/S3 ids appear under 2+ S1s. Enforce it as a hard constraint: each S2/S3 id goes to at most one S1 (the highest-scoring one). Keep it on (`decide.one_owner: true`).
- 26% of train S2/S3 records match no S1 (same share in every source × country), so unmatched distractors are common.

### Other facts that shape the pipeline
- Pairs never cross country (0.00%), so country-grouped blocking (dynamic groupby) loses nothing.
- Postal codes are rare: only 4.8% of pairs share a 5-6 digit token (93% when both sides have one). **House number + street tokens are the numeric key, not postal.**
- Cross-script names: India S2 23.5% and S3 13.2% are non-Latin (Devanagari, Telugu, Kannada, Tamil, Bengali, Gujarati, Malayalam). The address is the anchor there; that side needs multilingual embeddings (Dhyanesh).
